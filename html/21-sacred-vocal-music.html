Music History

Renaissance

2.1. Sacred vocal music

The motet and the mass

Religious music took on an international character. The most prominent composers were appointed as chapel masters at the major cathedrals and travelled constantly throughout Europe.

  • The main genre of religious music was the motet, a piece for several voices (usually four or five), based on a religious text in Latin and with a predominantly polyphonic texture, which employed the technique of imitative counterpoint.
  • The other religious musical genre is the Mass. It was composed by setting the most important prayers of this service to music, namely the Kyrie eleison (Lord, have mercy), Gloria, Credo, Sanctus and the Agnus Dei (Lamb of God).

Some notable composers included Josquin des Prez, Orlando di Lasso and Giovanni Perluigi da Palestrina. In Spain, Tomás Luis de Victoria (Ávila) and Cristóbal de Morales and Francisco Guerrero, both from Seville, stood out.
