2.3. Instrumental Music

The revival of instrumental music

During the Renaissance, the profile of instrumental music was much lower than that of vocal music; indeed, its role was relegated to providing a background to songs and dances. However, the situation changed in the sixteenth century, when instrumental music began to be written as compositions in their own right. At first, instrumental music drew on vocal genres such as the motet or the Chanson, adopting different names in the process: ricercare, canzona, etc. However, it wasn’t until the Baroque period that instrumental music came to be regarded as a completely separate entity.

Factors that influenced the development of instrumental music include:

  • Advances in the manufacture of instruments.
  • The more rapid publication of theoretical and practical treatises about instrumental music, enabled by the introduction of the printing press.
  • New systems of notation that did not require extensive musical knowledge (tablature).

Another significant change that influenced instrumental music composition was the use of variations (called diferencias in Spanish). The instrumentalist would overlay a given framework with variations on the original melody. An example of this is in the famous variations of Guárdame las vacas, by Luis de Narváez 

Luis de Narváez - Guárdame las vacas

Dance music was also very important during this period. It became the practice to have dances in pairs, the first dance being slow and the second one fast. This gave rise to common dance pairs such as the Pavane and the Galliard, or the Passamezzo and the Saltarello.
